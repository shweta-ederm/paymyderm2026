Executive Summary

Five things to know before reading the flows
Cards are validated twice, never charged, when added

Collect.js checks the card in the browser and tokenizes it; NMI validates again when saving to the customer vault. PMD never touches raw card numbers, only tokens.

Every payment follows the same two-step spine

Charge through NMI first; only on success does PMD post the payment to eDerm. A failed eDerm posting never blocks the payment, since an hourly job retries it.

Payment plans self-heal across three attempts

Installments auto-charge on the due date, then retry on each of the next two days with patient emails in between. Three consecutive failures cancel the plan.

Plans create a temporary ledger mismatch

Once a plan starts, PMD shows a $0 balance while eDerm still shows the unpaid remainder. The same balance stays collectible from eDerm, PayMyDerm, or the front desk.

One decision is blocking: how to reconcile the two ledgers

If the plan-covered balance gets paid on the eDerm side, should eDerm flag it as covered by a PMD plan, or should such a payment cancel the PMD plan via a webhook? See Open Questions.

The core tension

How the Two Ledgers Look While a Plan Is Active

eDerm posting rules, and the balance mismatch that drives the open questions below.
Every paid amount posts to eDerm under the oldest unpaid charge (the same allocation eDerm's own Pay Now / PayMyDerm uses), with a description identifying it as a PayMyDoc payment. But when a payment plan is created, only the first installment has actually been paid, and the two systems now describe the same patient differently:

PayMyDoc shows…

  • Balance of $0. The plan absorbs the full amount owed.
  • An active payment plan with upcoming installments.
  • New charges from future appointments appear as fresh balance.

eDerm shows…

  • Only the first installment's amount as paid.
  • The remaining plan balance as unpaid.
  • No indication that a PMD payment plan covers it.
The exposure: double collection

Because eDerm still shows the remainder as unpaid, that same balance can be collected again (directly in eDerm, through PayMyDerm, or at the front desk) while the PMD plan keeps auto-charging the patient on schedule. Nothing currently connects the two.

Decision needed

Open Questions

Raised by the development team; blocking the payment-plan / eDerm reconciliation design.
The scenario: a patient has an active payment plan in PayMyDoc, so eDerm still shows the plan's remaining balance as unpaid. That balance can be paid from eDerm directly, from PayMyDerm, or at the front desk. What should happen to the PayMyDoc payment plan when that occurs?
Option 1

Clear the balance on eDerm and flag it

Mark the plan-covered balance as settled (or otherwise not collectible) on the eDerm side, with an identifier showing it will be covered by the PayMyDoc payment plan.

  • Removes the double-collection risk at the source; staff can't accidentally collect it again
  • The plan runs its course undisturbed
  • eDerm's ledger no longer reflects true unpaid A/R until installments post
  • Requires eDerm-side changes: a "covered by PMD plan" state and identifier
Option 2

Cancel the PMD plan when eDerm collects

Leave the eDerm balance open, and if it is paid from the eDerm side, automatically cancel the PayMyDoc payment plan so the patient isn't charged again.

  • eDerm's ledger stays accurate and existing front-desk workflows are unchanged
  • Patient is never double-charged after the eDerm-side payment lands
  • Balance remains visibly collectible in the meantime, so partial overlap is possible
  • Requires a webhook: eDerm must notify PMD whenever such a balance is paid
Worth confirming

Additional Observations

Not raised by the developer; points noticed while assembling this summary, worth confirming during review.
  • Failed one-time payments leave no trace for the patient. The error is shown only at the moment of payment: no email, no persistent notice. Plan installment failures do email the patient; consider whether one-time failures should too.
  • A $0 PMD balance during an active plan may confuse patients. The patient still owes the remaining installments, but the headline balance reads zero. Confirm the plan card/UI makes the outstanding plan amount unmistakable.
  • Partial overlap is possible under either option. If an installment charges the same day a front-desk payment is taken, a refund path will be needed. Worth defining who owns that refund (PMD via NMI, or the practice via eDerm).
  • Reminder timing wording. The spec says reminders go out "7 days before each later appointment"; assumed here to mean each installment due date. Worth confirming with the developer.
